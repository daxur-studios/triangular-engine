A 1 m ball (fixed size, independent of world size) dropped onto a buildColliderPatch() collider from /cell-planet-lab's M4d work, positioned in real meters and fed straight into Jolt's RVec3 double-precision path (see this page's own doc comment) instead of the unit-sphere fraction the original demo used. A test is placed automatically as soon as the planet finishes generating, and the camera snaps to a close view of it — no need to go hunting across the whole planet for a 1 m ball first. The magenta wireframe is the collider patch itself made visible (toggle above); the invisible Jolt shape is the exact same geometry. "move test to camera look-at" repositions it (and re-frames the camera to match); "zoom to planet" pulls back out to see the whole sphere. Switch world size tiers and watch the ball at "extra-large" especially — that is the real test of whether double precision is actually doing its job at planetary offsets, not float32 jitter/sinking through the floor.
