Meadow: gusting wind over grass + wildflowers

{{ variantCount }} grass clump variants and {{ wildflowerVariantCount }} wildflower variants (buildGroundCoverClumpMesh, one seed each — no branching skeleton, unlike flora) are each registered as their own scatter species on a finer identity cell than trees typically use, so density reads as a field. Wildflowers reuse grass's archetype schema and mesh builder — fewer, taller, straighter stems, each topped with a real bloom (archetype.head, two crossed quads at the stem tip colored independently of the stem's own green gradient, not just a tinted blade end) — proving a scatter layer isn't grass-specific: same placement, streaming (selectFixedLevelScatterCells), and instancing (buildScatterInstancedMesh) pipeline, unchanged from the flora-scatter-lab pattern, run twice with a different archetype and a much smaller candidate pool so wildflowers stay sparse accents rather than a second dense layer. Both are shape-agnostic already, so switching between the flat plane, a sphere, and the inside of a cylinder above needs no placement changes at all, only a different terrain domain + field per shape.

Every clump flutters on its own desynced timer (enableScatterWindSway, per-instance phase) — the same primitive flora-scatter-lab uses for trees. A gust (GRASS_WIND.gust) adds a second, coherent flow on top: curl noise (a smooth noise field's gradient, crossed with the local surface normal) sampled at world position and time, so nearby clumps bend together while the flow direction itself swirls and drifts instead of pointing one fixed way. The same noise field is also contrast-masked into patches with a visible boundary ("gust fronts"), so it reads as eddies drifting through the field rather than a uniform shimmer. Amplitude, eddy size, and drift speed are all live GPU uniforms (setGustAmplitude, setGustWavelengthM, setGustDriftSpeedMS). Wildflowers enable the same GRASS_WIND definition on their own material and their own wind handle — the sliders below call both handles, so one control visibly drives every species at once rather than each needing its own set.

Grass density fades out toward the view distance (generateTerrainScatterInstances's distanceFade) instead of rendering uniformly to the field's edge, and re-thins as the camera moves (ScatterStreamingService). A second, directional cull (scatter/core/scatter-view-cull, promoted out of this page so any game can use it) drops grass outside a conservative cone in front of the camera — a coarse angle-to-forward test, not a true 6-plane frustum, ported from brunos-space-program's cdlod-terrain-lab. Freeze it to detach the cull boundary from the live camera, then orbit/zoom freely to see exactly where it falls — on the flat plane that boundary is a literal circle when looking straight down (a symmetric cone sliced by a plane perpendicular to its axis), not a bug.

No-grass zones (scatter/core/scatter-exclusion, docs/runbook/018) prove a "no grass under this building" mechanism: a world-space circle suppresses density with a soft feathered edge, composing with the distance fade and view cull above — passed to both species' placement calls, so wildflowers are excluded from a zone too, not just grass. Circular, not rectangular — a world-space distance test needs no per-shape surface basis, so it works unmodified on all three shapes here. Permanently removing already-existing grass (a rocket engine scorching the ground) is a different, already-proven mechanism — scatter-removal-overlay, exercised end-to-end in scatter-physics-lab — not duplicated on this page.
